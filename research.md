    <h1>Research</h1>

    <p class="research-lead">
      My research focuses on
      <strong>visual working memory and visual cognition</strong>,
      with a particular interest in how
      <strong>interactions and representational structure</strong>
      shape visual representations and behavior.
    </p>

    <p>
      Visual representations do not exist in isolation.
      They are influenced by past experiences and decisions,
      constrained by relationships with other representations,
      and organized within structured representational spaces.
      I investigate how these interactions and structural
      properties shape visual memory and contribute to
      systematic biases in behavior.
    </p>

    <p>
      My work combines behavioral experiments,
      computational modeling, and neuroimaging to examine
      these questions at both behavioral and neural levels.
    </p>

  </header>

    <div class="research-area-number">
      01 / INTERACTIONS ACROSS TIME
    </div>

    <h2>History Effects in Visual Cognition</h2>

    <p class="research-question">
      How do past experiences and decisions
      shape current visual perception and memory?
    </p>

    <p>
      Our perception and memory of visual information
      are systematically influenced by previous experiences.
      A prominent example is serial dependence, in which
      current visual estimates are biased toward or away
      from previously encountered information.
    </p>

    <p>
      I investigate how these history effects depend on
      spatial context, previous decisions, and task relevance.
      My research examines the conditions under which
      information from the past influences current visual
      processing and how these influences differ between
      perception and working memory.
    </p>

      02 / INTERACTIONS ACROSS ITEMS
    </div>

    <h2>Relational Structure in Visual Working Memory</h2>

    <p class="research-question">
      How do relationships between remembered items
      constrain individual memory representations?
    </p>

    <p>
      Visual working memory is often studied in terms of
      how accurately individual items can be maintained
      and retrieved. However, remembered items may not
      be represented independently. Their relationships
      can provide additional structure that influences
      how individual values are estimated.
    </p>

    <p>
      I investigate how relational information is
      represented and used in visual working memory.
      In recent work, I found that feedback about one
      remembered item systematically shifts estimates
      of another item in a direction that preserves
      their original relationship.
      These findings suggest that the organization
      of memory contents can constrain the estimation
      of individual representations.
    </p>

      03 / REPRESENTATIONAL STRUCTURE
    </div>

    <h2>Representational Geometry and Visual Biases</h2>

    <p class="research-question">
      How does the geometry of neural representations
      relate to systematic biases in visual memory?
    </p>

    <p>
      Visual features are not necessarily represented
      uniformly in the brain. Even when stimulus values
      are evenly spaced in physical space, their neural
      representations may exhibit non-uniform distances
      and asymmetric relationships.
      Such structure may constrain how visual information
      is estimated and remembered.
    </p>

    <p>
      I investigate the geometry and dynamics of
      neural representations during visual working memory.
      In recent fMRI work, I found that asymmetries
      in the representational geometry of orientation
      in early visual cortex correspond to systematic
      behavioral biases. This correspondence is
      particularly pronounced during working memory
      maintenance and captures individual differences
      in memory bias.
    </p>

    <p>
      More broadly, I am interested in understanding
      how representational structure changes over time
      and how its organization relates to the
      systematic distortions observed in visual cognition.
    </p>

      ADDITIONAL RESEARCH
    </div>

    <h2>Neural Dynamics of Spatial Memory</h2>

    <p>
      Beyond visual working memory, I have investigated
      how large-scale neural dynamics support memory
      for navigational experiences.
      Using fMRI and inter-subject analyses, I examined
      shared patterns of cortical and hippocampal activity
      during naturalistic navigation and their relationship
      to subsequent spatial memory performance and aging.
    </p>
